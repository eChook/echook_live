eChookLive
UX Assessment Sept 2026

The app shows the car's data. It doesn't yet answer the pit wall's question.

The craft here is high: consistent tokens, dark mode done properly, lazy tabs, draggable cards, keyboard shortcuts, honest empty states, confirmations before data loss. What's missing is a layer above the channels. Greenpower is an endurance format run against a fixed energy allowance, and every screen in the app is organised around instantaneous values rather than the two questions a team actually asks: are we going to finish, and is anything about to break.

P1

Structural — worth changing the information architecture for

1 · There is no race, only a stream

DashboardHeader.vue · DashboardView.vue

The header's most prominent piece of state is Loaded Data: 02/09/26, 09:12 – Live — a data-plumbing fact. Races exist in the store (telemetry.races, startTimeMs, lapHistory) and are used to compute chart zoom, but never surfaced as context. Elapsed time, laps completed, energy consumed against allowance, and projected finish are the whole sport, and none of them have a home.

Fix: a persistent status summary — charge and energy delivered, consumption rate, and comparison against a reference the team enters. No projections: eChook cannot know pack capacity, and the tool shouldn't imply a strategy. The Data Homescreen is the corrected version.
Superseded: mockup 1 below still shows an allowance projection and a prescribed target speed, and mockups leaning on race clock and lap count. All wrong for this product — capacity is unknown, lap detection isn't yet robust, and strategy belongs to the team. Kept as a record of the discussion only.

2 · Every channel has identical visual weight

DataCard.vue · orderedKeys

The ribbon renders every available key as an identical 128px tile. Speed, motor temp, gear-ratio diagnostics and Wh used are typographically indistinguishable, so the eye has to read labels rather than recognise shapes. Drag-to-reorder is a genuinely good escape hatch, but it asks the user to build their own hierarchy — and the order they choose is flat either way.

Fix: tier the ribbon. Two or three pinned hero metrics at large size with a trend sparkline, the rest as a compact strip. Keep drag, but let it move cards between tiers — that's a more meaningful gesture than reordering equals.

3 · Alerting is reactive, not predictive

eventThresholds.js · useCriticalEventAlerts

Thresholds tint a card once a limit has already been crossed. In a 90-minute endurance race the actionable moment is several minutes earlier: motor temp climbing 2 °C/min, or consumption tracking 6% above the budget line. The data to do this is already in telemetry.history; only the framing is missing.

Fix: derive rate-of-change per channel and raise a single, sentence-shaped warning — "Motor temp +2.1 °C/min · reaches 95 °C in ~6 min" — with the offending channel one tap away. One banner, never a stack.

4 · One layout serves three different jobs

tabs (graph · map · laps · analytics · batteries · admin)

The rail mixes live race work (Graph, Map, Laps), post-race investigation (Analytics, Batteries) and administration in one flat list, with admin-only entries appearing inline for some users. During a race, half the rail is noise; after a race, the live ribbon is dead weight above the analysis. The two modes also want different refresh, pause and zoom semantics.

Fix: split Race from Analyse at the top level. Race shows the strategy strip, tiered ribbon, graph, map, laps. Analyse drops the ribbon, gains the session picker and the battery/analytics work. Admin moves out of the racing rail entirely.

P2

Interaction — cheap fixes, high daily value

5 · Chart zoom state is invisible

R, 1–9 and L set a zoom window with no on-screen trace. A user who pressed 3 two minutes ago can't tell whether the chart is following live or frozen on three old laps — and a frozen chart during a race is a wrong-decision machine.

Fix: a state chip — FOLLOWING LIVE vs LOCKED · LAST 3 LAPS — clickable to return to live.

6 · Pause is a global with a quiet indicator

Space toggles pause from anywhere, and the only feedback is a header icon state. A laptop on a pit table catches stray keystrokes; a paused dashboard looks exactly like a healthy one that has stopped moving.

Fix: give pause the same page-level treatment as the reconnecting pill, plus a dimmed ribbon and a resume affordance. Keep the shortcut; make the state impossible to miss.

7 · Shortcuts are taught once, then hidden

A first-run modal is the only place the shortcut set exists. Powerful bindings (tab cycling, pan, scale, lap zoom) end up used by whoever read the modal on day one.

Fix: a persistent ? affordance in the graph toolbar and inline key hints on the controls they drive. Discoverability at the point of use beats a modal.

8 · Loading history destroys the present

"Loading data for 02/09/26 will remove all currently loaded data" is an honest warning about a model that shouldn't need one. Comparing this morning's practice to this afternoon's race is the most natural thing a team wants to do, and the app treats it as a destructive swap.

Fix: sessions as named, switchable things — and eventually a ghost trace of a reference session under the live one. That deletes the modal instead of improving it.

9 · Stale data reads as styling

50% opacity plus grayscale is a tasteful stale treatment, but out of context it looks like a disabled control rather than "this number is old". The car status chip carries the age; the cards don't.

Fix: keep the fade and add an explicit age on the stale card itself — 12s. Old data that admits its age is safe; old data that looks live is not.

10 · Mobile pays the desktop's density tax

On a phone the ribbon becomes a long horizontal scroll of 128px tiles, so the three numbers a marshal or driver-changer needs are several swipes apart, and the bottom bar competes for the same thumb.

Fix: mobile shows the pinned tier only, full-width, with everything else behind one "All channels" sheet.

P3

Reach — audience and conditions

11 · Spectator mode reports, it doesn't narrate

The public view is a car table cross-linked to a map, showing speed. Spectators care about order, gaps and laps — the story of the race — and none of those are computed even though positions and lap data exist server-side.

Fix — revised: no positions or gaps. Only a minority of the field runs eChook, so any implied order would contradict the official results. Instead make the telemetry itself the draw: live speed, a moving trace on the map, and a persistent "unofficial · telemetry from participating cars only" note. Mockup below shows a ranked table and is superseded on this point.

12 · Light and dark, but no daylight

Themes are light / dark / system. The actual environments are a bright July paddock and a dim garage — and light mode's #71717a muted text on white is the first thing to disappear behind glare and sunglasses.

Fix: a third "Daylight" theme — pure white surfaces, near-black text, heavier borders, no muted grays below 4.5:1, larger metric type. Same tokens, one more block.
